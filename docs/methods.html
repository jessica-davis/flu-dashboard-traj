How the forecasts work

Each week the EpyStrain model forecasts flu hospital admissions in every state and nationally for the next four weeks, and when the season will start and peak.

The data

We use weekly counts of hospital admissions with laboratory-confirmed influenza reported to CDC's National Healthcare Safety Network (NHSN), as published in the FluSight forecast hub. Weeks run Sunday to Saturday. Recent weeks can still change as late reports arrive; the dashboard's Data as reported option shows the numbers as they stood when each forecast was made.

Lined up by week from early August. Click a season to show or hide it.

The forecast

The EpyStrain model, developed at Northeastern University, is fit to each location's weekly hospital admissions and projects them forward for the rest of the season. Because the future is uncertain, it makes 1,000 trajectories for every location, each a possible path the coming weeks could take.

For each week we line the 1,000 trajectories up from lowest to highest and read off quantiles. The 50th percentile is the median: half the trajectories are below it and half above. A prediction interval runs between two quantiles. The 50% interval runs from the 25th to the 75th percentile, and the 90% interval from the 5th to the 95th.

Activity and trend

Both weekly gauges work the same way. The chart is divided into blocks: activity levels, or trends compared with the last reported week. We count how many of the 1,000 trajectories land in each block in the forecast week. That count, out of 1,000, is the chance of each answer, and the gauge points to the result. The map colors every state by the same rule.

Season start projection

The season starts in the first week that flu hospital admissions reach the epidemic threshold from the Moving Epidemic Method, nationally admissions a week; each state has its own. Each trajectory has its own first week above the threshold. The chance the season has started by a given week is the share of the 1,000 trajectories that have crossed by then. Once reported admissions cross, the dashboard shows the week it happened.

Peak projection

The peak is the week with the most flu hospital admissions in the season. To forecast it, the same 1,000 trajectories behind the four-week forecast are extended past the fourth week to the end of the season. Each trajectory's highest week is its peak. The gauge shows the share of trajectories whose peak has already passed, and the likely dates hold the 10th to 90th percentile of the peak weeks.

How sure we are

How sure the model is shapes the wording of the overview at the top of the dashboard. It is the share of the 1,000 trajectories that give the most common answer: if 890 of them land in Low, 89% agree.

More than 75% agree
“Low activity”
e.g. 890 of 1,000 are Low
51% to 75% agree
“Likely Stable”
e.g. 620 of 1,000 are Stable
50% or less agree
“Low to Medium”
e.g. 460 are Low and 440 Medium; a split trend reads “Trend unclear”

For the trend, Increasing and Sharply Increasing count as the same answer, as do the two decreases.

The data

Flu hospital admissions
Hospital admissions with laboratory-confirmed influenza in one week, reported to NHSN, for each state, DC and the United States.
Week
Sunday to Saturday, named by its dates, for example Oct 18–24.
Data as reported
The numbers as they stood when a forecast was made, before later revisions, from the FluSight hub's weekly snapshots.
Previous seasons
Past flu seasons on the chart, lined up by week of the season.

The forecast

Forecast date
The Saturday a forecast is for. The model only knew about weeks before it.
Forecast week
One of the four weeks a forecast covers. The first ends on the forecast date.
Trajectory
One possible path the coming weeks could take. The model makes 1,000 for every location.
Quantile
The value below which a given share of the trajectories fall in a week. The 25th percentile has a quarter of them below it.
Median
The 50th percentile: half the trajectories are below it, half above.
Prediction interval
The range between two quantiles: 25th to 75th for the 50% interval, 5th to 95th for the 90% interval, 2.5th to 97.5th for the 95% interval.
Likely range
The 10th to 90th percentile (the 80% interval), shown in the dashboard's overview.

The gauges

Activity level
Low, Medium, High or Very High, set by each location's Moving Epidemic Method intensity thresholds in weekly admissions.
Trend
Sharply Decreasing, Decreasing, Stable, Increasing or Sharply Increasing: the change in the weekly rate per 100,000 people compared with the last reported week, with wider limits further ahead. A change of fewer than 10 admissions is always Stable.
Epidemic threshold
The Moving Epidemic Method level that separates the off-season from the flu season, nationally admissions a week. It is set separately from the activity levels.
Season start
The first week admissions reach the epidemic threshold.
Peak
The week with the most flu hospital admissions in the season, forecast by extending the trajectories to the end of the season.
Chance
How many of the 1,000 trajectories give an answer. "Low 89%" means 890 are Low.
Agreement
The share of trajectories giving the most common answer. Above 75% the overview names the answer; above 50% it says “Likely”; otherwise it gives a range or says the trend is unclear.

The epidemic and intensity thresholds come from the Moving Epidemic Method: Vega T, Lozano JE, Meerhoff T, et al. Influenza surveillance in Europe: establishing epidemic thresholds by the moving epidemic method. Influenza and Other Respiratory Viruses 2013;7(4):546–558. MEM is the threshold method recommended in WHO's Pandemic Influenza Severity Assessment (PISA) guidance and used by WHO Europe and ECDC.
